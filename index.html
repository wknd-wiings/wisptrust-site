The problem — facts, not fear

You already attest to this every year.

The requirement isn't new, and it isn't optional. What most small firms are missing isn't effort — it's a plan that describes their firm, built before the day it's needed.

01

You already attest to this every year

Form W-12 — the PTIN application and renewal every paid preparer files — line 11 asks you to acknowledge that paid tax return preparers are required by law to create and maintain a written information security plan. All PTINs expire December 31. Renewal season is when the question gets asked; the plan has to exist before the answer is given.

02

The requirement is size-blind

Tax preparers are financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (16 CFR 314.2(h)(2)(viii)). IRS Publication 5708 says the WISP requirement applies “regardless of size.” Firms under 5,000 consumers are exempt from four specific paragraphs of 16 CFR 314.4 — we explain them in writing to every client — but the program itself, the risk assessment it's based on, MFA, encryption, training, vendor oversight, and FTC breach notification apply to everyone.

03

A generic template describes a generic firm

The IRS's own guide says “there is no one-size-fits-all WISP” and that a plan should be developed from the specific needs of your business. A plan that claims safeguards you haven't actually turned on is worse than an honest gap list — after an incident, it's the first thing an insurer or investigator reads.

04

The day the plan matters is not a calm day

It's the day a laptop is stolen, an email account is taken over, or an insurer asks for your documentation. The clocks are short and they don't match each other: the IRS expects data theft reported to your stakeholder liaison by the next business day; the FTC must be notified within 30 days when 500 or more consumers' unencrypted information is acquired (16 CFR 314.4(j)); state deadlines run by where your clients live. A plan written in advance, with those steps pre-drafted, is the difference between a bad week and a catastrophe.
